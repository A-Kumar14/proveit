# Stage script: every kind of answer, in about six minutes

One student, one concept (**CI interpretation**, Intro Statistics), eight different answers. Each beat shows a different reaction from the same tutor. The lines in quotes are what you say or type. Every one was run against the live model while writing this, so the reactions below are what to expect. The exact wording changes from run to run. The behaviour does not.

Before you start: tap **Reset demo** in Settings, Data and privacy, check the microphone is allowed, and have `service.py` running if SignBridge is on the agenda.

## Beat 0. Frame it (0:20)

Open the dashboard. Point at the rank card and at Intro Statistics.

> "Mastery doesn't ask whether you got it right. It asks whether you can prove you understand. This student is about 230 points from Platinum, and has one open misconception on confidence intervals. Watch what the tutor does with each kind of answer."

Open **Intro Statistics**, tap **CI interpretation**, then **Test this concept**.

The tutor opens by asking what the student thinks. There is no question on screen and nothing is graded yet. Say so: *"Nothing here is graded. It's a conversation."*

## Beat 1. Fully wrong (0:30)

Type: **"It means 95% of all the data points fall inside the interval."**

What it does: says the idea is common, then corrects it without a lecture. It explains that the interval is a net trying to catch one fixed target, and that the 95% is about the net-making method. It ends with a question: *if we repeated this 100 times, how many intervals catch the true mean?*

Say: *"It doesn't say 'wrong'. It corrects, gives a picture, and hands the next step back to the student."*

## Beat 2. Lost (0:50)

Tap the **I don't know yet** chip.

What it does: does not teach yet. It asks which part is confusing and shows three choices, one of them **Teach me from scratch**. Tap that.

It then teaches from the beginning in small steps. A fixed true average sits out there, and an interval is a net cast to catch it. It checks the picture landed before moving on.

Type: **"Is it like a net that sometimes misses the fish?"**

What it does: confirms, and builds on the student's own picture. About 95 of 100 nets catch the fish. Then it asks what the chance is that the fish is in *one* net.

Say: *"For a student who is lost, it diagnoses first and teaches second. And it uses their words."*

## Beat 3. Half right (0:35)

Type: **"About 95 out of 100 would catch it. So for mine, there's a 95% chance it has the true mean."**

What it does: says the first sentence is spot on and credits it. It isolates the second sentence as the trap and fixes only that. It uses a coin that was already flipped and covered, and asks what the chance is that it landed heads.

Say: *"Half right gets half credit. It tells you which half."*

## Beat 4. Talks back (1:00)

Tap **Talk it through**, then the orb.

Say out loud: **"The 95% is about the method. If I built intervals this way over and over, about 95% of them would contain the true mean. Any one interval either contains it or it doesn't."**

What it does: answers by voice in a calm, short reply, and picks up from the chat, not from the start. Interrupt it halfway through a sentence by just talking. It stops and listens. Captions show on screen.

Tap **End call**, then **Back to chat**. The whole voice conversation is in the chat.

Because the answer was right, expect the tutor to say so and offer to check what the student has got, with **Check what I've got** and **Keep talking**.

Say: *"The same tutor works by voice and text. And it knows when the student is ready to be tested."*

If the voice tutor's wording differs, that's fine. The point is the interruption and the hand-back to the chat.

## Beat 5. Vague, then caught (1:30)

Tap **Check what I've got**. The screen shows one graded question: *Explain what a 95% confidence interval means.* Say: *"This is the only graded part."*

First, type the vague answer: **"I think it's about how confident we are in the estimate, but I'm not sure how."**

What it does: does not guess a misconception. It says it needs one more answer and asks a sharper question: *what do you think that 95% is actually telling us about it?*

Say: *"If it isn't sure, it says so. It never invents a mistake."*

Now type the real mistake: **"There's a 95% probability that the true mean is inside the interval we calculated."**

What it does: opens the diagnosis page. It names the misconception, **Parameter probability trap**. It quotes the student's own words. It explains the gap: *the true mean is fixed, and your interval either contains it or doesn't.* Three independent model votes agree, and the page shows 3 of 3.

Say: *"It found the exact misconception and showed the evidence for it."*

Continue to the short lesson and the figure.

## Beat 6. Proves it (0:40)

The next question is different from the first: *100 researchers each build a 95% interval. About how many contain the true mean, and what does that say about one interval?*

Optional, 15 seconds: type **"About 80 of them."** It fails and tells the student it should be about 95. Say: *"Failing here is fine. Nothing is lost."*

Type: **"About 95 of the 100 should contain the true mean. Once one interval is calculated, it either contains the fixed mean or it doesn't, so the 95% describes the method."**

What it does: passes. The concept moves to Provisional, and Intro Statistics crosses into **Platinum**, the account does too, and the tier-up celebration plays. (Checked on a fresh seed: course 2,977 to 3,300, account 9,777 to about 10,100.)

Say: *"Mastery moved because the student proved it on a question they hadn't seen. Not because they were told the answer."*

## Beat 7. Quiz (0:30)

Open **History**. Show the saved quiz (4 of 6). Open it for the audit.

What it shows: the right answers, the wrong ones, a left-blank question, and an explanation for each. The History line says what the student is right on and weak on.

Say: *"A quiz tells us where to look. It never proves mastery. Only a new question can."*

## Beat 8. Signs (0:40)

Open the course tutor (course map, then **Tutor**). Tap **Sign** in the prompt bar, then **Start camera**. Hold an ASL letter steady for a moment; it lands in the letter strip. Spell something short, such as **HI** or **CI**. Tap **Use this**, and the word is in the message box. Send it.

Say: *"A deaf student answers the same tutor with their hands. It spells letters, so short answers work best. It never sends until they say so."*

Needs `service.py` running (see the setup note at the top). If the service is off, the panel says the exact command to start it and the camera stays off.

## If something goes sideways

- **Model slow or down.** The tutor needs a key and takes 1 to 3 seconds a reply. Keep talking over the wait. If it errors, retry once. The screen puts your message back in the box.
- **Voice won't connect.** The red message on the page says why. Say you'll show it by text, go **Back to chat**, and keep going. Nothing else depends on it.
- **A different reaction than this script.** Say what it did. The behaviour matters: correct kindly, credit the right half, never invent a mistake, grade only on request.
- **Need to redo it.** Open Settings, Data and privacy, and tap **Reset demo**. It reloads the starting state in a second.

## What this proves to the judges, in one sentence each

1. It reacts to the answer, not a script: wrong, half right, lost, vague, right.
2. It teaches before it tests, and tests only when asked.
3. It won't guess: vague answers get a follow-up, not a verdict.
4. When it finds a mistake, it names it and shows the quote.
5. Progress moves only on proof from a new question.
6. The same tutor talks, listens, and can be interrupted.
