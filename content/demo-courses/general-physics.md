# General Physics

A first course in mechanics. It describes how things move, explains why with forces, and then uses energy and momentum as shortcuts for problems that forces make hard.

## Motion

### Displacement, velocity and acceleration
Displacement is the change in position, including direction. Velocity is how fast displacement changes, and acceleration is how fast velocity changes. Speed is the size of velocity without direction, so an object moving in a circle at constant speed is still accelerating because its direction changes.

### Constant acceleration
When acceleration is constant, velocity changes by the same amount each second and position follows a parabola in time. The kinematic equations link initial velocity, final velocity, acceleration, time and displacement, so any three known values give the other two.

### Projectile motion
A projectile moves horizontally at constant velocity while it accelerates downward at g, about 9.8 metres per second squared. The two directions are independent, so a ball dropped and a ball thrown sideways from the same height hit the ground at the same time.

## Forces

### Newton's first and second laws
An object keeps its velocity unless a net force acts on it. The second law says net force equals mass times acceleration, so the same push gives a light object a larger acceleration. Forces add as vectors, and only the net force sets the acceleration.

### Newton's third law
When one object pushes on another, the second pushes back with a force equal in size and opposite in direction. The pair act on different objects, which is why they do not cancel out each other's motion.

### Friction and normal force
The normal force is the push of a surface, perpendicular to it. Friction acts along the surface against sliding or attempted sliding, and its maximum size is a coefficient times the normal force. Static friction can be anything up to that maximum, kinetic friction is roughly constant once sliding starts.

## Energy and momentum

### Work and kinetic energy
Work is force times displacement along the force. The work-energy theorem says the net work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy, one half m v squared, so doubling speed quadruples kinetic energy.

### Conservation of energy
With no friction or other losses, kinetic plus potential energy stays constant. Gravitational potential energy near the Earth is m g h, so height turns into speed on the way down. With friction, the missing mechanical energy becomes heat.

### Momentum and collisions
Momentum is mass times velocity, and with no external force the total momentum of a system stays the same. In every collision momentum is conserved, while kinetic energy is conserved only in elastic collisions, and in a perfectly inelastic one the objects stick together.
