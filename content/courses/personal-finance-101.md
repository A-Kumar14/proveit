# Personal Finance 101

A practical first course on managing money. It covers what comes in and goes out, how credit and debt work, how money grows, and how taxes and retirement accounts fit around it.

## Budgeting and safety

### Budgeting basics
A budget is a plan that compares income with spending before the month happens. A simple version splits take-home pay into needs, wants and savings, for example 50, 30 and 20 percent. The point is not the exact split. It is knowing where money goes so spending follows choices instead of habit.

### Emergency fund
An emergency fund is cash set aside for surprises such as a job loss or a medical bill. A common target is three to six months of essential expenses. It belongs in an account that is safe and easy to reach, such as a high-yield savings account, not in investments that can fall in value when the money is needed.

## Credit and debt

### Credit scores
A credit score summarises how reliably someone repays borrowed money. In the US, payment history and the share of available credit in use matter most, followed by the length of credit history, new applications and the mix of accounts. Paying on time and keeping balances well below limits raises a score over time.

### Debt payoff
There are two common orders for paying off several debts. The avalanche method pays the highest interest rate first and costs the least in total interest. The snowball method pays the smallest balance first, which gives quick wins that help some people stay on track. Either way, every debt gets its minimum payment and the extra money goes to one target.

## Growing money

### Compound interest
Compound interest means earning interest on past interest as well as on the original amount. A balance growing at a steady rate rises faster each year, which is why starting early matters more than starting big. The same effect works against borrowers, because unpaid interest on a debt can itself start charging interest.

### Index funds
An index fund holds all the stocks in a market index instead of trying to pick winners. It spreads risk across many companies and charges very low fees. Over long periods, low fees matter because fees come out of returns every year, and most professionally managed funds fail to beat their index after costs.

## Taxes and retirement

### Taxes 101
In a progressive income tax system, rates are marginal. Each rate applies only to the slice of income inside its bracket, so moving into a higher bracket does not tax all earnings at the higher rate. Deductions lower the income that is taxed, and credits reduce the tax owed directly. Withholding on a paycheck is an advance payment toward the year's tax bill.

### Retirement accounts
Retirement accounts give tax breaks for long-term saving. A traditional 401(k) or IRA takes contributions before tax and taxes withdrawals later. A Roth account takes after-tax money and allows tax-free withdrawals in retirement. Many employers add matching contributions to a 401(k), which is effectively extra pay for saving.
